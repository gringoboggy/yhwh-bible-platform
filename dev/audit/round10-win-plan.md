## Executive summary

This round-10 deep audit produced **11 deduped findings → 8 verified survivors (3 refuted)**; of the survivors, **6 are bug/correctness/debt findings and 2 are optimization recommendations**, with severity distribution **{high: 1, medium: 1, low: 5, info: 1}**. The most serious is the **HIGH** cache-discipline gate failure: a new `@lru_cache` on `_estimate_kepub_aside_bytes` is neither whitelisted nor cleared, turning three `test_audit_caches` tests red — a red commit gate, not a content defect. The one **MEDIUM** is a real byte-stability hole: `kindle_post.py` re-zips with bare-string `writestr`, stamping wall-clock `date_time` into every member so the M4 Kindle catalog assets are not reproducible across the v1.0.0 re-cut. The remaining five LOWs are stale config/test drift and dev-only Kobo tooling decay, none of which touch the shipped Ethiopian artifact's content. Overall codebase health is strong: no silent data loss, no security exposure, the 9 KJV editions remain byte-stable, and every finding is additive or test/dev-only — the engine and the marathon core are untouched.

### Recurring-invariant note (lint vs pytest)
The cache-discipline invariant (Phase 1 HIGH) recurs on **every** new `@lru_cache`, so the commit-time check is the right home — `scripts/audit_caches.py` already runs as that lint and `tests/test_audit_caches.py` mirrors it; keep the whitelist the single source of truth rather than relying on a pytest-only guard. The byte-stability invariant (Phase 2) should remain enforced by the determinism unit test plus the regen-and-`git diff` obligation; a commit-time `lint_rules` check that flags any `writestr(<str>, …)` in OCF packagers (i.e. requiring a pinned `ZipInfo`) would prevent the whole class from recurring — recommended as a follow-up backstop.
